## Quest briefing

### Problem

Photos and files were piling up on my laptop with no dedup, no tagging and
no backup, while a Raspberry Pi already running my personal services sat
mostly idle. I wanted a personal cloud I control, without opening my home
network to the internet.

![Raspberry Pi in its case, cabled up next to a keyboard](/images/projects/homelab/1761096851370.webp)

### What I built

- **Reproducible from code.** The Pi is provisioned with **Ansible (11
  roles)**: Docker, HDD mount, Samba, Immich, n8n, Tailscale, monitoring,
  Watchtower and more. The AWS side (IAM, S3, DynamoDB, Lambda) is
  **Terraform**.
- **Event-driven AI photo pipeline.** Photos sync from the local HDD to S3.
  Each upload triggers a Lambda that tags the photo (Rekognition) and
  embeds it (AWS Bedrock Titan), storing results in DynamoDB. Scripts on
  top of that do duplicate detection and text-to-photo search.
- **Immich** as the self-hosted photo app, replacing iCloud with no storage
  cap or subscription.
- **Zero inbound ports.** Public services, including this portfolio, are
  exposed only through outbound **Cloudflare Tunnels**. Private services
  (the NAS, Immich, Grafana) are reachable only on the LAN or over
  **Tailscale**. Samba never touches the tunnel.
- **Ops.** Containers run under Portainer, Watchtower keeps images updated
  and posts to a Discord channel, and Grafana dashboards cover the Pi,
  each container and the AWS side, with disk alerts before a mount fills.

### Result

- **14,000+ photos (300+ GB)** moved off iCloud onto hardware I own.
- **0 inbound ports** open on the home network, and you're reading this
  page through the same tunnel.
- The whole box can be rebuilt from the repo instead of from memory.

## Loot (what was learned)

- Dedup surfaced organizational debt, not just stray copies: most
  duplicates were whole folders mirrored into each other.
- Raw object counts lie. Filter to the files you'll actually process
  before sizing the job.
- Outbound-only tunnels remove an entire class of attack surface.
- ARM quirks: not every container image loves a Raspberry Pi.
- DNS is always the problem. Even at home. *Especially* at home.
